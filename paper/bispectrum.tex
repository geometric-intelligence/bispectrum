\begin{abstract}
Many machine learning tasks are invariant under the action of a group $G$ of transformations: signal classification
can be invariant under translations, image
classification under 2D rotations, and spherical-image classification under 3D rotations. The $G$-bispectrum is a \emph{complete} invariant of a signal (it determines the signal up to the group action) and has been used as a pooling layer in deep networks. However, the full bispectrum is costly to compute, and each existing implementation covers a single group.
We present \texttt{bispectrum}, an open-source, fully unit-tested
PyTorch library that implements \emph{selective} $G$-bispectra for seven group actions as differentiable modules. For finite groups $G$, selectivity
reduces the computational cost from $O(|G|^2)$ to $O(|G|)$. For planar rotations, we use the selective disk bispectrum. For spherical 3D rotations,
we introduce an augmented selective bispectrum at \llm{M03a}{band-limit}{Spelling drift: ``band-limit'', ``bandlimit'' and ``band limit'' all occur as the noun (eleven marks, M03a--k). (A) ``band limit'' for the noun and ``band-limited'' for the adjective everywhere. (B) ``bandlimit'' everywhere.}
$L$ which reduces the cost \llm{M11}{from $O(L^3)$ to $\Theta(L^2)$}{Mixes $O$ and $\Theta$ for the same comparison; the contributions say $\Theta(L^3)$ to $\Theta(L^2)$. (A) ``from $\Theta(L^3)$ to $\Theta(L^2)$''.} coefficients.
We profile the library, showing that it is invariant up to floating-point error, with its selective $G$-bispectra computed in sub-millisecond time on GPU (up to band limit $L{=}16$ on the sphere). We evaluate selective $G$-bispectra as pooling layers in $G$-equivariant networks on PatchCamelyon ($C_8$) and OrganMNIST3D (the octahedral group $O$), against norm pooling,
gated pooling, Fourier-ELU pooling, max pooling, and a (non-equivariant)
data-augmented CNN. Across 46 comparisons, bispectral pooling is never significantly worse than any of them; it is on par on PCam and improves on max pooling by 5.0\,pp on OrganMNIST3D.
On Spherical MNIST, the augmented selective bispectrum with an MLP head reaches 95.8\% on rotated test digits, on par with a matched spherical CNN at $4\times$ lower latency and within 0.3\,pp of a tensor-product network at $364\times$ lower latency.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In many learning problems the label does not change when a group $G$ of transformations acts on
the input: a translated signal, a rotated image, or a rotated spherical image keeps its class.
Equivariant neural networks~\citep{cohen2016group, weiler2019general} enforce invariance by adding an invariant pooling layer to their equivariant feature maps.
Existing choices (norm pooling, gate-based pooling, max/sum
aggregation~\citep{weiler2019general}) are simple but
\emph{incomplete}: they discard information that a complete
group-invariant representation would preserve. For instance, norm pooling retains only the power spectrum of the feature map, so two signals with the same per-frequency magnitudes are mapped to the same invariant
descriptor even when their relative phases differ and no group element relates them.

The $G$-bispectrum~\citep{giannakis1989bispectrum, kakarala2012bispectrum,
sanborn2023bispectral, mataigne2024selective, myers2025selective}, a complex-valued
$G$-invariant descriptor, avoids this. It is \textit{complete} (it preserves all signal
information up to the group action), \textit{invertible} (the signal can be recovered from
it up to a group action), and \textit{selective} (a small subset of its entries suffices for
completeness on generic signals). Selectivity was first observed for translations
by~\citet{giannakis1989bispectrum} and recently extended to finite groups and the
disk~\citep{mataigne2024selective,myers2025selective}.

\paragraph{Prior implementations.} The selective $G$-bispectrum
of~\citet{mataigne2024selective} covers signals defined over finite groups. The selective disk bispectrum from
\citet{myers2025selective} proposes a bispectrum for 2D images defined over the disk, with invariance with respect to planar rotations. The spherical bispectrum of \citet{kakarala2012bispectrum} provides a representation of spherical images that is invariant to 3D rotations; \citet{oreiller2020solid} use it as a locally rotation-invariant layer in 3D CNNs, keeping all $\Theta(L^3)$ coefficients up to a maximal degree~$L$, and \citet{chevalley2024bispectral} build a bispectral layer into a 3D U-Net for segmentation. The classical translation bispectrum~\citep{giannakis1989bispectrum} is well-known in signal processing but lacks an open, GPU-ready implementation. \citet{sanborn2023bispectral} learn the bispectrum of compact commutative groups from data with bispectral neural networks; this form does not cover non-commutative groups.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We present \texttt{bispectrum}, an open-source PyTorch library of
\emph{selective} $G$-bispectra. Our contributions are:

\section{Mathematical Background}
\label{sec:background}

Given a group $G$, a \emph{representation} $\rho$ assigns each $g\in G$ a
unitary matrix $\rho(g)$ with $\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h)$; an
\emph{irreducible representation} (\emph{irrep}) has no nontrivial
invariant subspace, and the \emph{dual} $\hat{G}$ is the set of equivalence
classes of irreps. A $G$-action on a domain $X$,
$(g,x)\mapsto g\cdot x$, induces an action on signals
$(f\circ g)(x):= f(g\cdot x)$; two signals are in the same \emph{orbit}
if and only if they are related by some $g\in G$, and an \emph{invariant}
is a function constant on each orbit.
A neural network layer $\Phi$ is \emph{$G$-equivariant} if
$\Phi(\pi_{\mathrm{in}}(g)\, f) = \pi_{\mathrm{out}}(g)\, \Phi(f)$
for all $g \in G$~\citep{cohen2016group, weiler2019general}.
Stacking equivariant layers preserves equivariance, but any task requiring
a prediction independent of the group action must eventually map equivariant
features to invariant ones.

\paragraph{Bispectrum on finite groups.}
The $G$-bispectrum~\citep{kakarala1992triple, mataigne2024selective}
provides a complete invariant for signals on finite
groups, usable as a pooling layer. Given a signal $\Theta : G \to \R$ on a finite group $G$ with
unitary irreps $\{\rho_i\}_{i\in\hat{G}}$, the \emph{$G$-Fourier
transform}~\citep{diaconis1990fourier} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-finite}
  \mathcal{F}(\Theta)_{\rho_i}
  := \sum_{g \in G} \Theta(g)\, \rho_i(g)^{\dagger}
  \;\in\; \C^{d_i \times d_i},
\end{equation}
where $d_i = \dim(\rho_i)$. The \emph{full $G$-bispectrum} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bispectrum-def}
  \beta(\Theta)_{\rho_1,\rho_2}
  = \bigl[\mathcal{F}(\Theta)_{\rho_1} \otimes
    \mathcal{F}(\Theta)_{\rho_2}\bigr]\,
    C_{\rho_1,\rho_2}\,
    \Bigl[\bigoplus_{\rho \in \rho_1 \otimes \rho_2}
      \mathcal{F}(\Theta)_{\rho}^{\dagger}\Bigr]\,
    C_{\rho_1,\rho_2}^{\dagger},
\end{equation}
where $C_{\rho_1,\rho_2}$ is the unitary Clebsch--Gordan matrix that
block-diagonalizes the tensor product $\rho_1 \otimes \rho_2$ into
irreducible summands. Each matrix
$\beta(\Theta)_{\rho_1,\rho_2}$ is $G$-invariant, and the full collection
$\{\beta(\Theta)_{\rho_1,\rho_2}\}_{(\rho_1,\rho_2)\in\hat{G}^2}$
is a complete $G$-invariant for generic signals (i.e.\ those with
nonsingular Fourier coefficients)~\citep{kakarala1992triple}.
The full bispectrum has $O(|G|^2)$ scalar entries.
\citet{mataigne2024selective} shows that a breadth-first search over
the Kronecker product table of $\hat{G}$ selects $O(|G|)$ bispectral
coefficients that remain complete; for example, the octahedral group
drops from $576$ to $172$ scalars.

\paragraph{Bispectrum on the disk.}
The disk bispectrum~\citep{zhao2014rotationally, myers2025selective}
is an $\SO(2)$-invariant descriptor for planar images defined on the
unit disk \llm{M06a}{$\mathbb{D}$}{Disk notation drift: $\mathbb{D}$ here, $D^2$ in the Figure 5 caption. (A) $\mathbb{D}$ everywhere. (B) $D^2$ everywhere.}. A signal $f : \mathbb{D} \to \R$ is expanded in
disk harmonics (eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on $\mathbb{D}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk-harmonics}
  \psi_{nk}(r,\theta) = c_{nk}\, J_n(\lambda_{nk}\, r)\, e^{in\theta},
\end{equation}
where $J_n$ is the Bessel function of order $n$, $\lambda_{nk}$ its
$k$-th positive root, and $c_{nk}$ a normalization constant.
The disk harmonic (DH) coefficients $a_{n,k}$ are equivariant to planar
rotations: if $f' = f \circ R_\phi$ then
$a^{f'}_{n,k} = e^{in\phi}\, a^{f}_{n,k}$.
For a \llm{M03c}{bandlimit}{``bandlimit'' (see M03a).} yielding $m$ coefficients with maximum angular frequency
$N_m$, the \emph{full disk bispectrum}~\citep{zhao2014rotationally} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bispectrum-disk}
  b_{j_1,j_2,k_3}
  = a_{n_{j_1},k_{j_1}}\, a_{n_{j_2},k_{j_2}}\,
    a_{n_{j_1}+n_{j_2},\,k_3}^{*},
\end{equation}
with $O(m^3 / N_m)$ coefficients. \citet{myers2025selective} introduces
the \emph{selective disk bispectrum}, restricting to two families of
triples that yield $O(m)$ coefficients while remaining a complete
$\SO(2)$-invariant (under the genericity condition $a_{n,1}\neq 0$ for
all angular orders $n$).

\paragraph{Bispectrum on the sphere.}
The spherical bispectrum~\citep{kakarala2012bispectrum} is an
$\SO(3)$-invariant descriptor for band-limited signals on $S^2$.
A real signal $f : S^2 \to \R$ at band limit $L$ has spherical harmonic
coefficients $a_\ell^m$ with coefficient vectors
$\mathbf{F}_\ell = (a_\ell^{-\ell},\ldots,a_\ell^{\ell})^\top
\in \C^{2\ell+1}$, transforming under $g\in\SO(3)$ as
$\mathbf{F}_\ell \mapsto D^\ell(g)\,\mathbf{F}_\ell$, where $D^\ell$
is the $(2\ell{+}1)$-dimensional Wigner $D$-matrix.
For each admissible triple $(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell)$ satisfying the
triangle inequality
$|\ell_1 - \ell_2| \le \ell \le \ell_1 + \ell_2$, the
\emph{bispectral coefficient} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bispectrum-sphere}
  \beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}
  = \sum_{\substack{m_1,\,m_2,\,m \\ m_1+m_2=m}}
    \CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}\;
    a_{\ell_1}^{m_1}\,a_{\ell_2}^{m_2}\,\conj{a_\ell^m},
\end{equation}
where $\CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}$ denotes the
Clebsch--Gordan coefficient
$\langle \ell_1, m_1;\, \ell_2, m_2 \mid \ell, m \rangle$.
The full set has $\Theta(L^3)$ scalars. On homogeneous spaces such as
$S^2$, the Fourier coefficients are rank-deficient (vectors rather than
full-rank matrices), so the operator-bispectrum completeness
of~\citet{kakarala2012bispectrum} does not directly
apply~\citep{kondor2007novel}. In Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective} we
introduce a selective construction that reduces the scalar bispectrum to
$\Theta(L^2)$ entries and test its completeness empirically by
signal reconstruction.

Throughout this paper, we use the term \emph{$G$-bispectrum} to refer
collectively to the bispectra defined by
Equations~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-def},~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-disk},
and~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-sphere}.




\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Equivariant network libraries.}
The \texttt{escnn}~\citep{cesa2022program} and \texttt{e3nn}~\citep{geiger2022e3nn}
libraries provide general-purpose equivariant convolution layers for 2D and 3D
groups respectively, and \texttt{lie-nn}~\citep{batatia2023general}
provides general Lie-group representation primitives (irreducible
representations, Clebsch--Gordan decompositions, tensor products) on
top of which custom equivariant layers can be built. These libraries
implement equivariant \emph{feature maps} but leave the choice of
invariant pooling to the user (typically norm or gate). Our modules provide the invariant map and work with any of these libraries.

\paragraph{Bispectral approaches in neural networks.}
\citet{sanborn2023bispectral} introduced bispectral neural networks (BNNs),
which learn a Fourier transform and its full bispectrum from data for compact
commutative groups (2D cyclic translations, planar rotations).
\citet{oreiller2022bispectral} build a locally rotation-invariant layer for a
2D U-Net from the bispectrum of local circular-harmonic responses, over all
frequency pairs up to a maximal degree; \citet{oreiller2020solid} do the same
in 3D with spherical harmonics, and \citet{chevalley2024bispectral} build such
a layer into a 3D U-Net for segmentation. Both Oreiller layers keep every
bispectral coefficient up to symmetry, and each of these implementations is
tied to a specific architecture. Our library
covers seven group/domain pairs, defaults to the
$O(|G|)$ selective reduction~\citep{mataigne2024selective}, and exposes
each invariant as a standalone PyTorch module.
Outside the equivariant-network setting,
\texttt{PyBispectra}~\citep{binns2025pybispectra} computes bispectrum
and bicoherence statistics of one-dimensional time-series signals for
electrophysiology applications such as phase-amplitude coupling and
time-delay estimation; the underlying 1D bispectrum is translation-invariant
by construction, but the toolbox is not designed as a neural-network
pooling layer.

\paragraph{Bispectra on homogeneous and non-homogeneous spaces.}
The selective $G$-bispectrum
construction~\citep{mataigne2024selective} reduces the cost of the
$G$-bispectrum from $O(|G|^2)$ to $O(|G|)$ scalars on finite groups.
\citet{myers2025selective} extends the selective construction to the
disk, where $\SO(2)$ acts on a non-homogeneous space.
\citet{kakarala2012bispectrum} treats the $\SO(3)$-on-$\SO(3)$
bispectrum and the closely related sphere case via Wigner-$D$ irreps.
The matrix (vector) bispectrum on $S^2$ is the homogeneous-space form
on which our scalar contractions and CG power augmentation are built.

\paragraph{Invariant theory and scattering.}\llmpt{C16}{The header promises invariant theory, but the paragraph covers only scattering. (A) \textbf{Scattering.} (B) add a sentence on invariant theory (polynomial invariants, orbit separation).}
The wavelet scattering transform~\citep{mallat2012scattering} provides
translation-invariant representations via iterated modulus operations;
\citet{mcewen2022scattering} extend it to the sphere. Unlike the
$G$-bispectrum, scattering representations are stable but generally
incomplete; they discard inter-scale phase information.

\section{The \texttt{bispectrum} Library}
\label{sec:library}

\paragraph{Design and implementation.} \label{sec:design} Every $G$/domain pair is a \texttt{torch.nn.Module} named
\texttt{\{Group\}on\{Domain\}} (e.g.\ \texttt{SO3onS2});
Table~\ref{tab:supported-groups} lists all seven modules. Each exposes a
uniform API: \texttt{forward(f)} returns the selective invariants,
\texttt{fourier(f)} the group Fourier coefficients, and
\texttt{invert(beta)} the reconstructed signal up to group-action
indeterminacy (where available). Modules default to $O(|G|)$
selective coefficients (\texttt{selective=False} restores the full
$O(|G|^2)$ set where available, Table~\ref{tab:supported-groups}), precompute CG matrices / DFT kernels / Bessel
roots as non-learnable buffers, and depend only on
PyTorch~\citep{paszke2019pytorch}, NumPy~\citep{harris2020numpy},
and \texttt{torch\_harmonics}~\citep{bonev2023spherical} (for
\texttt{SO3onS2}). Test suite line coverage is 98.5\%; a minimal
usage example is in Appendix~\ref{app:api-example}.

\paragraph{Profiling.} \label{sec:benchmarks} We profile the library on a single NVIDIA H100 80\,GB GPU
(PyTorch 2.11, \texttt{bispectrum} 0.4.1, batch\,=\,16 for the forward pass and 4 for inversion,
median wall-clock via \texttt{torch.utils.benchmark}); results are in
Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}.
With autograd enabled, as in training, the selective forward pass is sub-millisecond for all six modules,
from 0.08\,ms ($\mathbb{T}^2$) to 0.68\,ms (octahedral group). \texttt{SO3onS2} at $L{=}16$
takes 0.63\,ms, or 0.20\,ms in inference mode, where it replays a CUDA graph.
GPU throughput exceeds $10^7$~samples/s for $C_n$ and $\mathbb{T}^2$
(Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). The same configurations stay sub-millisecond on an A100, a consumer RTX 4090

\section{The Augmented Selective Bispectrum on $S^2$}
\label{sec:so3-selective}

We construct the augmented selective bispectrum
$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$, an
$\SO(3)$-invariant of size $\Theta(L^2)$ on band-limited spherical
signals, and probe its completeness empirically.

\paragraph{Setup and construction.} As in Section~\ref{sec:background}, a real signal
$f : S^2 \to \R$ band-limited at degree~$L$ has coefficient vectors
$\mathbf{F}_\ell \in \C^{2\ell+1}$, $\ell=0,\dots,L$, that transform as
$\mathbf{F}_\ell \mapsto D^\ell(g)\,\mathbf{F}_\ell$ under $g\in\SO(3)$. The reality
condition leaves $(L{+}1)^2$ real degrees of freedom (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-setup}), and the
full scalar bispectrum~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-sphere} has $\Theta(L^3)$ entries. The orbit space
$\R^{(L+1)^2}/\SO(3)$ has dimension $(L{+}1)^2 - 3$; by a rank
argument, any smooth complete $\SO(3)$-invariant must therefore have
at least $\Omega(L^2)$ components
(Proposition~\ref{prop:app-lower-bound}). 

\paragraph{The augmented selective bispectrum.} We define
$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}(f)$ as the output of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:selective-bsp-so3}. It has two parts: a
\emph{bispectral part} $\mathcal{S}_\beta$, a selected subset of the
coefficients~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-sphere}, and a \emph{CG-power part}
$\mathcal{S}_P$ of scalars
$P_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell} := \lVert(\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1}\otimes
\mathbf{F}_{\ell_2})|_\ell\rVert^2$, the squared norms of
Clebsch--Gordan projections (Definition~\ref{def:app-cg-power}).
Both are built degree by degree. Degrees $\ell\le 4$ contribute
22 bispectral entries and degrees $5\le\ell\le 7$ fixed explicit
blocks (Table~\ref{tab:Tsmall}). Each degree $\ell\ge 8$ contributes
up to $2\ell{+}1$ entries $\beta_{a,b,\ell}$ with $1\le a\le b<\ell$,
one per unordered triple, since on real signals all orderings of a
triple give the same real equation; at $\ell\in\{8,9\}$, where only
16 such triples are nonzero, the power entry $\beta_{0,\ell,\ell}$
and self-couplings fill the remainder. Every degree $\ell\ge 3$ also
receives the self-couplings $\beta_{\ell,\ell,\ell'}$ for even
$\ell'\le\ell$ (odd $\ell'$ gives a repeated-index, odd-parity entry
that vanishes identically on real signals;
Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}) and a fixed family of at
most seven CG-power scalars. With these included, the per-degree real
Jacobian of $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ has full rank $2\ell{+}1$, which
the test suite checks for $4\le\ell\le 20$ and a verification script
for $\ell\le 100$. The total size is $\Theta(L^2)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:app-output-size}): $380$ (resp.\ $426$) real
scalars at $L=15$ (resp.\ $16$), against $1{,}124$ (resp.\
$1{,}341$) entries of the full bispectrum
(Table~\ref{tab:app-counts}).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Index set of $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$, degree by degree. The
  low-degree and CG-power details are in
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented}
  (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-selective-index}).}
\label{alg:selective-bsp-so3}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} \llm{M03d}{band-limit}{``band-limit'' (see M03a).} $L$
\STATE $(\mathcal{S}_\beta,\mathcal{S}_P) \leftarrow$ entries of
  degree $\le\min(L,4)$ \COMMENT{22 bispectral, 10 CG-power at $L\ge4$}
\FOR{$\ell = 5, \dotsc, L$}
  \STATE $\mathcal{B}_\ell \leftarrow \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{small}}_\ell$
    if $\ell\le 7$, else $(a,b,\ell)$ for $1\le a\le b<\ell$,
    $a{+}b\ge\ell$, by $a{+}b$ then $a$
  \STATE append to $\mathcal{S}_\beta$ the first $2\ell{+}1$ entries of
    $\mathcal{B}_\ell$, $(0,\ell,\ell)$, $(\ell,\ell,\ell')$ for even
    $\ell'<\ell$ descending, that do not vanish on real signals
  \STATE append $(\ell,\ell,\ell')$ to $\mathcal{S}_\beta$ for even
    $2\le\ell'\le\ell$, if not present
  \STATE append the fixed CG-power triples of degree $\ell$ to
    $\mathcal{S}_P$ \COMMENT{5 for even $\ell$, 7 for odd}
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{\beta_t(f)\}_{t\in\mathcal{S}_\beta}\cup
  \{P_t(f)\}_{t\in\mathcal{S}_P}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Empirical evidence of completeness.}
\label{sec:so3-recon}
We call $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ a \emph{complete} $\SO(3)$-invariant if
$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}(f) = \Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}(g)$ implies that $f$ and $g$ lie in the same
orbit. Completeness can be probed empirically by attempting to invert $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$:
if it is complete, any
signal $\hat f$ matching $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}(f)$ must agree
with $f$ up to a global rotation, so consistent recovery of
the input is evidence that no orbit information is lost.
We run this test at the classifier's band limit $L{=}15$, where
$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ has 380 coefficients, on 8 Spherical MNIST digits
(each in its original and one random orientation) and 16 random
band-limited signals, sampled on a $128{\times}256$ grid. A
degree-by-degree solver works on the 256 real spherical-harmonic
coefficients: Levenberg--Marquardt with the exact Jacobian fits
degrees 0--4 from random starts, then adds one degree at a time and
refits all degrees so far. Each solution is rotated onto its target
before the relative image error is measured
(Appendix~\ref{app:so3-recon}).

$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is generically complete.


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the selective $G$-bispectrum as the invariant pooling layer of $G$-equivariant
networks on two benchmarks, 2D histopathology under $C_8$ and 3D organ classification under
the octahedral group $O$ (Section~\ref{sec:pooling}), and the augmented selective bispectrum
on spherical digit classification (Section~\ref{sec:smnist}).

\subsection{$G$-bispectral pooling in $G$-CNNs}
\label{sec:pooling}
\label{sec:pcam}
\label{sec:organ3d}\llmpt{M08}{Three labels on one subsection (sec:pooling, sec:pcam, sec:organ3d). (A) keep \texttt{sec:pooling} and point the other references to it.}

\paragraph{Setup.}
PatchCamelyon~\citep{veeling2018rotation} (PCam) has 327K $96{\times}96$ histopathology
patches with binary metastasis labels; every model uses a $C_8$-equivariant
DenseNet~\citep{huang2017densenet} backbone following \citet{veeling2018rotation}, and we
report test AUC. OrganMNIST3D~\citep{medmnistv2} has 1{,}742 abdominal CT volumes ($28^3$
voxels, 11 classes, 971 for training); every model uses an $O$-equivariant 3D ResNet with
exact voxel-permutation kernels, and we report test accuracy. Within a dataset the
equivariant models differ only in the terminal invariant map over the group axis: a maximum
(Max pool, and the NormReLU, Gated and Fourier-ELU backbones), the $\ell_2$ norm (Norm pool),
or the selective $G$-bispectrum (\texttt{CnonCn} with $n{=}8$, 128 coefficients;
\texttt{OctaonOcta}, 172). The bispectral head applies only fixed injective maps (real and
imaginary parts, signed logarithm) before a learned $1{\times}1$ projection and a ReLU. A
non-equivariant CNN trained with augmentation over $G$ (Aug.\ CNN) is the baseline without
built-in symmetry. Equivariant models are trained on canonical data only and tested on the
test set transformed by every element of $G$ (the \emph{rotated test}). Every model is trained
with AdamW~\citep{loshchilov2019adamw} for a fixed number of epochs that ends with a cosine
cycle~\citep{loshchilov2017sgdr}, without early stopping, and evaluated at its
best-validation checkpoint. We vary the training-set size
(PCam: 100 to 262K; OrganMNIST3D: 50 to 971) and the width (PCam: ${\sim}30$K to 1.6M
parameters; OrganMNIST3D: 0.37M to 6.3M for the equivariant models), over 3 to 10 seeds
(Appendices~\ref{app:pooling-grid} and~\ref{app:exp}).

\paragraph{Results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pooling-grid} shows the results. Across 46 seed-level comparisons
(Welch $t$-tests on the rotated test: every baseline at every training-set size, and at every
width on OrganMNIST3D; Appendix~\ref{app:pooling-grid}),
bispectral pooling is never significantly worse than any baseline. On OrganMNIST3D it
improves on max pooling on the same backbone by 5.0\,pp at full data
($78.5\%$ vs.\ $73.6\%$, $p<10^{-3}$); its smallest model (0.46M parameters) beats max
pooling's largest (6.0M) by 2.6\,pp; and with 500 training examples it beats max pooling
trained on all 971 by 3.6\,pp. It is 3.6 to 16\,pp above norm pooling and the augmented 3D
CNN at every training-set size from 100 and at every width. The advantage over max pooling
vanishes at the smallest training sets ($n\le250$: $-0.3$ to $+1.6$\,pp, not significant) and is not significant at

these tests have 80\% power only for gaps of about 0.025 AUC. All equivariant models score the same on the
canonical and rotated tests of OrganMNIST3D, where the 24 rotations permute voxels exactly.
Placing the ReLU before the projection instead, as in our submitted head, zeroes every
negative coefficient and costs 4--5\,pp on OrganMNIST3D (Appendix~\ref{app:changes}).

\subsection{Spherical MNIST: $\SO(3)$ invariance on the sphere}
\label{sec:smnist}

The pooling experiments cover finite groups. For a continuous group, we test \texttt{SO3onS2} (the augmented selective

MNIST~\citep{cohen2018spherical}, the standard benchmark for
rotation invariance on $S^2$.

\paragraph{Setup.}
MNIST digits are stereographically projected onto a

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have presented \texttt{bispectrum}, a PyTorch library that makes
selective $G$-bispectral invariants practical, covering seven
group actions through one \texttt{nn.Module} interface.
We contribute the augmented selective bispectrum, a $\Theta(L^2)$ $\SO(3)$ invariant on
$S^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective}), which a degree-by-degree solver inverts on every
signal we tested; we conjecture that it is generically complete. The selective forward pass
takes under a millisecond on GPU for every module at batch 16. As a pooling layer in
$G$-CNNs, the selective $G$-bispectrum is never significantly worse than norm, gated,
Fourier-ELU or max pooling or an augmented CNN; it is on par on PCam and improves on max
pooling by 5.0\,pp on OrganMNIST3D. On Spherical MNIST, the augmented selective bispectrum with an
MLP head is within 0.3\,pp of matched learned-equivariant networks on rotated digits, at
$4\times$ to $364\times$ lower latency. We speculate that the gain grows with how much
information incomplete pooling discards: per channel, norm pooling keeps one value per irrep,
5 of 8 for $C_8$ but 5 of 24 for $O$, and max pooling keeps 1 of $|G|$. Within the pooling head, a ReLU before the first learned
projection costs 4--5\,pp on OrganMNIST3D (Appendix~\ref{app:changes}).

\paragraph{When not to use the bispectrum.}\llmpt{C12}{Blog-style header. (A) merge into Limitations and future work. (B) \textbf{Scope.} (C) keep.}
Each module is invariant to a \emph{specific} group only; data augmentation or
flexible-symmetry equivariant networks fit better when the relevant symmetry is multi-group or
unknown. On the sphere, a fixed invariant followed by an MLP is less sample-efficient than a
learned spherical CNN.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
The degree-by-degree inversion of the augmented selective bispectrum is in our experiment code and not yet in the library, and a formal completeness proof, a characterization of the degenerate set,
and numerical-conditioning analysis at large \llm{M03e}{band-limits}{``band-limits'' (see M03a).} remain open. On $S^2$ we compare
against one tensor-product network, not against MACE~\citep{batatia2022mace} or wavelet
scattering, and use the invariant with an MLP head; bispectral pooling at the end of an
equivariant spherical backbone is future work.

\section{Augmented Selective Bispectrum on $S^2$ -- Structural Background}
\label{app:so3-proof}

This appendix gives the structural background for the construction
of Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective}: the $S^2$ band-limited setup and
notation (Section~\ref{sec:app-setup}); the orbit-space dimension
lower bound $(L{+}1)^2-3$ that motivates the $\Theta(L^2)$ target
(Section~\ref{sec:app-lower-bound}); the index set as implemented
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented}) and its size
(Section~\ref{sec:app-selective-index}); and the parity structure of
the scalar bispectrum that determines which triples vanish
identically on real signals
(Section~\ref{sec:app-parity}). Section~\ref{sec:app-comparison} compares the augmented selective bispectrum with prior
bispectral constructions, and Section~\ref{sec:app-pixel-complexity} counts its cost in
pixel space. We do not claim a completeness proof; generic completeness
is a conjecture, supported by the full per-degree Jacobian rank and by
the reconstructions of Figure~\ref{fig:so3-reconstruction}.

\subsection{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:app-setup}

\begin{definition}[Band-limited signal on $S^2$]
A signal $f : S^2 \to \R$ is \emph{band-limited} at degree $L$ if its
spherical harmonic expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-sh-expansion}
  f(\theta, \varphi)
  = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L} \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
    a_\ell^m \, \SH_\ell^m(\theta, \varphi)
\end{equation}
has $a_\ell^m = 0$ for all $\ell > L$. We write
$\mathbf{F}_\ell = (a_\ell^{-\ell}, \dotsc, a_\ell^{\ell})
  \in \C^{2\ell+1}$
for the coefficient vector at degree~$\ell$.
\end{definition}

\noindent
For real $f$, the conjugation symmetry
$a_\ell^{-m} = (-1)^m \conj{a_\ell^m}$ holds, so
$\mathbf{F}_\ell$ has $2\ell+1$ real degrees of freedom.
The total signal dimension is
$\dim = \sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(2\ell+1) = (L+1)^2$.

\begin{definition}[Rotation action]
Under $g \in \SO(3)$, the spherical harmonic coefficients transform as
$\mathbf{F}_\ell \mapsto D^\ell(g)\,\mathbf{F}_\ell$,
where $D^\ell$ is the $(2\ell+1)$-dimensional Wigner~$D$-matrix.
\end{definition}

\noindent
In the irrep notation of~\citet{mataigne2024selective},
$\{\rho_\ell\}_{\ell\ge 0}$ are the (unitary) irreducible
representations of $\SO(3)$ with $V_{\rho_\ell}$ the
$(2\ell{+}1)$-dimensional space of degree-$\ell$ spherical harmonics
and $\rho_\ell(g)=D^\ell(g)$; the Fourier coefficient of $f$ at
$\rho_\ell$ is $\mathcal{F}(f)_{\rho_\ell}=\mathbf{F}_\ell$. We use
$\mathbf{F}_\ell$ as the working symbol throughout.

\begin{definition}[Bispectrum on $S^2$]\label{def:app-bispectrum}
For each triple $(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell)$ satisfying the triangle
inequality $\abs{\ell_1 - \ell_2} \le \ell \le \ell_1 + \ell_2$,
the \emph{bispectral coefficient} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-bispectrum}
  \beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}
  = \sum_{\substack{m_1,\,m_2,\,m \\ m_1+m_2=m}}
    \CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}\;
    a_{\ell_1}^{m_1}\,a_{\ell_2}^{m_2}\,\conj{a_\ell^m},
\end{equation}
where $\CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}$ denotes the
Clebsch--Gordan coefficient
$\langle \ell_1, m_1;\, \ell_2, m_2 \mid \ell, m \rangle$.
Each $\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$ is a scalar; for real~$f$ it is
real when $\ell_1{+}\ell_2{+}\ell$ is even and purely imaginary when
$\ell_1{+}\ell_2{+}\ell$ is odd (Lemma~\ref{lem:app-parity}).
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:app-bispectrum} adapts the $G$-bispectrum~\eqref{eq:bispectrum-def}
to $\SO(3)$ acting on~$S^2$: the irreps are Wigner-$D$ matrices, the Fourier coefficients are the vectors
$\mathbf{F}_\ell$ rather than square matrices, and contracting with the Clebsch--Gordan
coefficients leaves one scalar per triple.

\begin{definition}[CG power spectrum]\label{def:app-cg-power}
For a triple $(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell)$ satisfying the triangle
inequality, write
$(\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1} \otimes \mathbf{F}_{\ell_2})\big|_\ell$
for the projection of the Kronecker product
$\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1} \otimes \mathbf{F}_{\ell_2}$ onto the
$D^\ell$-isotypic component, \llm{M04a}{realised}{British spelling; the rest of the paper is American. (A) ``realized''.} by contracting with the CG
coefficients $\CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}$. The
\emph{CG power spectrum entry} is the squared norm of this projection,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-cg-power}
  P_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}
  := \lVert (\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1} \otimes \mathbf{F}_{\ell_2})|_\ell
     \rVert^2
  = \sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
    \Bigl\lvert \sum_{\substack{m_1,m_2 \\ m_1+m_2=m}}
      \CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}\;
      a_{\ell_1}^{m_1}\, a_{\ell_2}^{m_2} \Bigr\rvert^2.
\end{equation}
This is a degree-$4$, real-valued, $\SO(3)$-invariant polynomial in
the signal coefficients. The CG-power part $\mathcal{S}_P$ of the
augmented selective bispectrum is a fixed family of these scalars, at
most seven per degree and $O(L)$ in total ($77$ at $L=15$), following
a pattern found by greedy augmentation at $L\le 8$ and extended to
all degrees (Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented}). With them included,
the per-degree real Jacobian of $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ has full rank
(Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective}).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Size of the full bispectrum]\label{prop:app-full-size}
The number of admissible triples $(\ell_1, \ell_2, \ell)$ with
$0 \le \ell_1, \ell_2, \ell \le L$ satisfying the triangle inequality
$|\ell_1 - \ell_2| \le \ell \le \ell_1 + \ell_2$ is $\Theta(L^3)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$, the triangle inequality admits
$\min(\ell_1 + \ell_2, L) - |\ell_1 - \ell_2| + 1$ valid values of
$\ell$. Summing over $(\ell_1,\ell_2)\in[0,L]^2$ gives
$N_{\mathrm{full}} = \Theta(L^3)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Invariance]
Each $\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$ is $\SO(3)$-invariant: the Wigner
matrices satisfy $D^{\ell_1} \otimes D^{\ell_2}
= \bigoplus_\ell D^\ell$ under the CG decomposition, so the triple
product contracted with CG coefficients is unchanged by
$\mathbf{F}_\ell \mapsto D^\ell(g)\mathbf{F}_\ell$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Why $\mathcal{O}(L)$ is impossible}\llmpt{M07}{$\mathcal{O}$ here, $O$ everywhere else. (A) $O(L)$.}
\label{sec:app-lower-bound}\llmpt{C13}{Header ``Why ... is impossible'': rhetorical question-style header. (A) ``Lower bound on the output size''. (B) ``A dimension lower bound''.}

\begin{proposition}[Dimensional lower bound]\label{prop:app-lower-bound}
Any \emph{smooth or polynomial} complete $\SO(3)$-invariant for
band-limited signals on $S^2$ at \llm{M03f}{band-limit}{``band-limit'' (see M03a).} $L$ must have codomain
of dimension at least $(L+1)^2 - 3$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi : \R^{(L+1)^2} \to \R^k$ be a smooth (or polynomial)
$\SO(3)$-invariant map that separates orbits on a \llm{X01}{Zariski-open
dense subset}{Completeness pass: ``Zariski-open'' applied to a smooth, not necessarily polynomial, map. (A) ``an open dense subset''.} $U\subset\R^{(L+1)^2}$. Smoothness implies $\Phi$ has a
well-defined generic rank $r := \max_{x\in U} \mathrm{rank}\,
d\Phi_x$; on the open dense subset where this rank is attained,
$\Phi$ is locally a submersion onto an $r$-dimensional submanifold,
and orbit-separation forces $r \ge \dim(\R^{(L+1)^2}/\SO(3))
= (L+1)^2 - \dim\SO(3) = (L+1)^2 - 3$

$k \ge r \ge (L+1)^2 - 3 = \Omega(L^2)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The selective index set}
\label{sec:app-selective-index}

This subsection describes the index set as implemented in \texttt{bispectrum.so3\_on\_s2}, which
Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented} mirrors. The \emph{degree}
of an entry is its largest index.

\begin{definition}[Augmented selective bispectrum]\label{def:app-selective}
The \emph{augmented selective bispectrum} $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ at
\llm{M03g}{band-limit}{``band-limit'' (see M03a).} $L$ consists of a bispectral part $\mathcal{S}_\beta$
(entries $\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$) and a CG-power part
$\mathcal{S}_P$ (entries $P_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$,
Definition~\ref{def:app-cg-power}), assembled degree by degree.
Degrees $\ell\le\min(L,7)$ use the fixed entries of
Table~\ref{tab:Tsmall}. Each degree $\ell\ge 8$ receives
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item the first $2\ell{+}1$ entries, among those that do not vanish
  identically on real signals
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}), of the list: the
  triples $(a,b,\ell)$ with $1\le a\le b\le\ell{-}1$ and
  $a{+}b\ge\ell$, ordered by $a{+}b$ and then by $a$; the power entry
  $(0,\ell,\ell)$; and the self-couplings $(\ell,\ell,\ell')$ for even
  $\ell'<\ell$ in descending order;
\item every even self-coupling $(\ell,\ell,\ell')$,
  $2\le\ell'\le\ell$, not already selected;
\item five (even $\ell$) or seven (odd $\ell$) CG-power triples
  $(\ell_1,\ell,\ell')$ with $\ell_1\le 3$
  (Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented}).
\end{enumerate}
At $\ell\in\{8,9\}$ the list in (i) has only 16 nonzero triples
$(a,b,\ell)$, so the power entry and self-couplings make up the remaining entries. From $\ell=10$ on, the triples alone supply
all $2\ell{+}1$. Only one ordering
of each triple is listed because, on real signals, all orderings of a
triple are proportional
(Remark~\ref{rem:app-Tell-rank-deficit}).

Mathematically, $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}:V_L\to\R^N$, where
$V_L\cong\R^{(L+1)^2}$ is the space of real signals band-limited
at~$L$, has $N$ real components: by
Lemma~\ref{lem:app-parity}, each bispectral entry contributes one
real component on real signals (real for even parity,
purely imaginary for odd), and CG-power entries are real-valued
degree-$4$ invariants. The PyTorch implementation stores each
scalar in a complex tensor, so downstream layers receive $2N$ real
channels after splitting real and imaginary parts; for bispectral
entries exactly one of the two channels is identically zero by
parity, and for CG-power entries the imaginary channel is
identically zero.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Parity structure of the bispectrum}
\label{sec:app-parity}

The selective construction of Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective}
exploits two structural facts about the scalar bispectrum
$\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$: a parity rule that fixes whether each
entry is real or purely imaginary on real signals (Lemma~\ref{lem:app-parity})
and the resulting identical vanishing of certain odd-parity entries
with a repeated index (Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}). With a permutation identity, the lemma fixes how many distinct real equations the
bispectral entries of each degree supply (Remark~\ref{rem:app-Tell-rank-deficit}). The
proposition fixes which candidate entries Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented} discards.

Let $T_R$ be the reflection $(T_R\cdot f)(\theta,\varphi) :=
f(\theta,-\varphi)$ through the $xz$-plane, an element of
$\mathrm{O}(3)\setminus\SO(3)$. In the Condon--Shortley convention
behind the reality condition
$a_\ell^{-m}=(-1)^m\conj{a_\ell^m}$, it acts on coefficients as
$a_\ell^m\mapsto(-1)^m a_\ell^{-m}$.

\begin{lemma}[Parity of the bispectrum]\label{lem:app-parity}
For any signal (real or complex):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-parity}
  \beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(T_R \cdot f)
  = (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell}\,\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f).
\end{equation}
For \emph{real} signals, there is an independent constraint:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-reality-constraint}
  \conj{\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f)}
  = (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell}\,\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f).
\end{equation}
Together:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
\item If $\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell$ is even:
  $\beta$ is real-valued (by~\eqref{eq:app-reality-constraint})
  and $T_R$-invariant (by~\eqref{eq:app-parity}).
\item If $\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell$ is odd:
  $\beta$ is purely imaginary (by~\eqref{eq:app-reality-constraint})
  and $\beta(T_R \cdot f) = -\beta(f)$, so $\im\beta$ flips sign under $T_R$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under $T_R$, each $a_\ell^m \mapsto (-1)^m a_\ell^{-m}$.
Substituting into $\beta = \sum_m (\sum_{m_1+m_2=m} C\, a^{m_1} a^{m_2})
\conj{a^m}$, the three sign factors $(-1)^{m_1+m_2+m} = (-1)^{2m} = 1$
cancel. Re-indexing $m \mapsto -m$ and applying the CG symmetry
$\CG{\ell_1}{-m_1}{\ell_2}{-m_2}{\ell}{-m}
  = (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2-\ell}\,\CG{\ell_1}{m_1}{\ell_2}{m_2}{\ell}{m}$
yields~\eqref{eq:app-parity} (with $(-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2-\ell} = (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell}$).

For real signals, $\conj{a_\ell^m} = (-1)^m a_\ell^{-m}$.
Taking the complex conjugate of $\beta$ and re-indexing similarly
yields~\eqref{eq:app-reality-constraint}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[CG power is $T_R$-invariant]\label{lem:app-P-invariant}
For all triples $(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell)$:
$P_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(T_R \cdot f) = P_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Under $T_R$, the CG-projected component transforms as
$(\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1} \otimes \mathbf{F}_{\ell_2})|_\ell^M
  \mapsto (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell+M}\,
  (\mathbf{F}_{\ell_1} \otimes \mathbf{F}_{\ell_2})|_\ell^{-M}$.
Taking the squared norm and re-indexing the sum over $M$
leaves $P$ unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Vanishing of odd-parity entries with a repeated index]
\label{prop:app-odd-vanishing}
Let $\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}$ be the scalar bispectrum entry of a
real signal $f$ (so $\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell$ odd). If the index multiset
$\{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell\}$ contains a repeated value, then
$\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f)\equiv 0$ as a polynomial in the SH
coefficients of $f$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Define the symmetric scalar 3j contraction
\[
  T_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3}(f) :=
  \sum_{m_1,m_2,m_3}
  \begin{pmatrix} \ell_1 & \ell_2 & \ell_3 \\
                  m_1 & m_2 & m_3 \end{pmatrix}\,
  a_{\ell_1}^{m_1}\, a_{\ell_2}^{m_2}\, a_{\ell_3}^{m_3} .
\]
Converting the Wigner~3j into a \llm{M09}{Clebsch-Gordan}{Single hyphen; elsewhere ``Clebsch--Gordan''. (A) ``Clebsch--Gordan''.} coefficient via
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} j_1 & j_2 & j_3 \\
       m_1 & m_2 & m_3\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)
= \frac{(-1)^{j_1-j_2-m_3}}{\sqrt{2j_3+1}}\,
  \CG{j_1}{m_1}{j_2}{m_2}{j_3}{-m_3}$ and applying the reality identity
$a_{\ell_3}^{m_3} = (-1)^{m_3}\,\conj{a_{\ell_3}^{-m_3}}$ shows
\[
  \beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f)
  = (-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2}\sqrt{2\ell+1}\,
    T_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}(f),
\]
so $\beta$ and $T$ have the same vanishing locus. The 3j symbol is
invariant under even column permutations and picks up
$(-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3}$ under odd ones. If two columns $i,j$
satisfy $\ell_i=\ell_j$, the inserted vectors $a_{\ell_i},a_{\ell_j}$
are literally the same coefficient vector of $f$, so swapping those
two columns leaves the contracted sum $T$ unchanged while multiplying
the 3j prefactor by $(-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell_3}$. For odd parity this
forces $T=-T$, hence $T\equiv 0$ and $\beta\equiv 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Identically-zero candidates]
\label{rem:app-Tell-zero-rows}
Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing} rules out, for example,
$(\ell,\ell,\ell')$ with odd $\ell'$, $(2,3,2)$, $(4,5,4)$ and
$(6,7,6)$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented} discards every such
candidate, including the rows $(4,5,4)$ and $(6,7,6)$ of the library's
explicit blocks (Table~\ref{tab:Tsmall}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[First nonzero odd-parity entries]\label{cor:app-first-odd}
The first odd-parity entries with all-distinct indices, and hence not
covered by Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}, occur at
degree~$4$: $\beta_{2,3,4}$ and its orderings $\beta_{2,4,3}$ and
$\beta_{3,4,2}$. They are not identically zero (a random real signal
gives a nonzero value), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:app-parity} they change
sign under $T_R$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No parity-breaking at $\ell \le 3$]\label{rem:app-no-parity-breaking-le3}
For degrees $\le 3$, every odd-parity triple $(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell)$
has at least two indices equal (since $\max(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell) \le 3$
and $\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell$ odd forces at least one pair to be equal in this range).
By Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}, all odd-parity entries vanish.
Therefore, the $T_R$ ambiguity cannot be resolved using only degrees $\le 3$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Distinct equations per degree]
\label{rem:app-Tell-rank-deficit}
On real signals each bispectral entry is one real number
(Lemma~\ref{lem:app-parity}), and all orderings of a triple are
proportional: $\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}
=(-1)^{\ell_1+\ell_2+\ell}\beta_{\ell_2,\ell_1,\ell}$ by the CG
exchange symmetry, and
$\beta_{\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell}=(-1)^{\ell_1}
\sqrt{(2\ell+1)/(2\ell_2+1)}\,\beta_{\ell_1,\ell,\ell_2}$ by the
3j form in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:app-odd-vanishing}.
Only distinct unordered triples therefore count toward the $2\ell{+}1$
real unknowns of $\mathbf{F}_\ell$, and for $\ell\ge 8$
Algorithm~\ref{alg:so3-augmented} lists one ordering per triple. At
$\ell\in\{8,9\}$ only 16 such triples with $a,b<\ell$ are nonzero,
so the power entry and self-couplings make up the remaining entries. The explicit
blocks at $4\le\ell\le 7$ (Table~\ref{tab:Tsmall}) list several
orderings of some triples and supply 4, 4, 8 and 8 distinct
bispectral equations from their 10, 10, 13 and 14 nonzero rows. The
submitted $\ell\ge 8$ block listed two orderings of the same triples
(Appendix~\ref{app:changes}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Complexity in pixel space}
\label{sec:app-pixel-complexity}

A signal band-limited at degree~$L$ is sampled on an equiangular grid
of $P = \Theta(L^2)$ pixels (e.g.\ $34\times 68$ at $L=16$ in
Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks}), so $L=\Theta(\sqrt{P})$. The augmented
selective bispectrum has $\Theta(L^2)=\Theta(P)$ entries, linear in the
number of pixels, against $\Theta(L^3)=\Theta(P^{3/2})$ for the full
bispectrum. Each entry contracts up to $O(L^2)$ CG-weighted products
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-bispectrum}), so the forward contraction as
implemented costs $O(L^4)=O(P^2)$ for $\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ and
$O(L^5)=O(P^{5/2})$ for the full bispectrum. Counting the terms of the
contraction at $L=64$ ($128\times 256$ grid): the full bispectrum has
$70{,}257$ entries and $2.67\times 10^{8}$ terms, and
$\Phi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ has $5{,}610$ entries and $1.08\times 10^{7}$
terms, a $25\times$ reduction. Between $L=32$ and $L=64$ the term
counts grow with local exponents $4.86$ and $3.71$.

\section{$G$-Bispectral Pooling: Extended Results}
\label{app:pooling}

\subsection{Invariant maps in $G$-CNNs (Figure~\ref{fig:pooling-grid})}
\label{app:pooling-grid}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The rotated test averages the metric over the test set transformed by every element of $G$,
identity included. On OrganMNIST3D the 24 octahedral rotations permute voxels exactly, so
$G$-invariant models score identically on the canonical and rotated test sets; on PCam the
$45^\circ$ rotations are bilinearly interpolated, so invariance is exact only at multiples of
$90^\circ$. Aug.\ CNN is a non-equivariant CNN (3D for OrganMNIST3D) trained with augmentation
over $G$, so the rotated test is in-distribution for it. All other models are $G$-equivariant,
trained on canonical data only, and end in an invariant map over the group axis: a maximum (Max
pool; NormReLU, Gated and Fourier-ELU name the backbone nonlinearity), the $\ell_2$ norm (Norm
pool, an incomplete invariant), or the selective $G$-bispectrum (complete). Max pool and
Bispectrum on OrganMNIST3D, and Norm pool and Bispectrum on PCam, share a ReLU backbone and differ
only in the invariant map; OrganMNIST3D's Norm pool uses NormReLU.

\paragraph{Held fixed.}
Left: full training set, at the smallest width (OrganMNIST3D) or ${\sim}100$K-parameter
configurations (PCam). Middle: full training set (OrganMNIST3D) or $n = 12.5$K (PCam). Right:
smallest width (OrganMNIST3D) or ${\sim}100$K parameters (PCam); ``all'' is the full training set,
and the PCam axis is broken before it (262K examples). The panels of a row share the $y$-axis.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation over 10 seeds (OrganMNIST3D) and 3 seeds (PCam; 5 at
$n \le 2.5$K). $p$-values are two-sided Welch $t$-tests, uncorrected for multiplicity. The 46
comparisons are 25 on PCam (5 training-set sizes, 5 baselines) and 21 on OrganMNIST3D (4
training subsets at the smallest width and the full training set at 3 widths, 3 baselines);
the PCam width sweep is not tested, because the methods' parameter counts differ at each
growth rate. On
OrganMNIST3D, bispectral pooling beats max pooling by 5.0\,pp ($p < 10^{-3}$); its smallest
model (463K parameters) beats max pooling's largest (6.0M) by 2.6\,pp
($p < 10^{-3}$); and at $n = 500$ it beats max pooling at $n = 971$ by
3.6\,pp ($p = 0.0017$). On PCam, no model is significantly better than bispectral
pooling at any training-set size; the closest is Gated on the full training set (${+}0.016$ AUC,
$p = 0.093$). With 3 seeds per arm and PCam's seed spread, these tests have 80\% power only for
gaps of about 0.025 AUC, so ``on par'' means no difference at that resolution.

\subsection{Model variants}

\paragraph{PCam.}
All equivariant models share a $C_8$-equivariant
DenseNet~\citep{huang2017densenet} backbone with block configuration
$(4, 4, 4)$, 24 initial channels, and compression factor 0.5,
following the architecture of \citet{veeling2018rotation} but
replacing the final invariant map. Six model variants are evaluated:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Aug.\ CNN}: non-equivariant DenseNet trained with augmentation over $C_8$
    and global average pooling. No group dimension, so ${\sim}8\times$ fewer parameters at
    matched growth rate.
  \item \textbf{NormReLU}, \textbf{Gated}, \textbf{Fourier-ELU}: $C_8$-equivariant
    backbones with norm-based, gated (doubling internal channels) or Fourier-space ELU
    nonlinearities, each followed by max pooling over the group dimension.
  \item \textbf{Norm pool}: ReLU backbone, $\ell_2$ norm over the group dimension.
  \item \textbf{Bispectrum}: ReLU backbone, selective $G$-bispectrum via
    \texttt{CnonCn}$(n{=}8)$ as the terminal invariant map.
\end{itemize}
The data-efficiency and full-data panels use growth rates matched to ${\sim}100$K
parameters. The parameter-efficiency panel sweeps the DenseNet growth rate
$k \in \{3, 4, 6, 8, 12\}$ for equivariant models and $k \in \{6, 12, 20, 30, 35\}$ for
Aug.\ CNN at $n = 12.5$K, from ${\sim}30$K to ${\sim}1.6$M parameters.

\paragraph{OrganMNIST3D.}
All equivariant models share an $O$-equivariant 3D ResNet whose kernel rotations are exact
voxel permutations on the $3^3$ grid. The channel widths $(4, 8)$, $(8, 16)$ and $(16, 32)$
give 463K, 1.7M and 6.3M parameters with the bispectral head (172 \texttt{OctaonOcta}
coefficients per channel) and 374K, 1.5M and 6.0M with max pooling. Aug.\ CNN is a
non-equivariant 3D CNN (16K, 63K and 251K parameters) trained with one of the 24 octahedral rotations per sample; Norm pool
uses a NormReLU backbone. Training-set sizes are $n \in \{50, 100, 250, 500, 971\}$ at
width $(4, 8)$.

\subsection{Data preprocessing}

\paragraph{PCam.}
PatchCamelyon~\citep{veeling2018rotation} consists of 327{,}680 RGB
patches of size $96 \times 96$ pixels extracted from whole-slide
images of lymph node sections~\citep{bejnordi2017camelyon}. We use
the standard train/val/test split. Images are loaded from HDF5 files
and \llm{M04b}{normalised}{British spelling (see M04a). (A) ``normalized''.} to $[0, 1]$. Aug.\ CNN is trained with augmentation over $C_8$; all
equivariant models are trained without augmentation. Training-set sizes
$n \in \{100, 500, 2.5\mathrm{K}, 12.5\mathrm{K}\}$ are uniform random subsets of the 262K
training patches, drawn per seed.

\paragraph{OrganMNIST3D.}
OrganMNIST3D~\citep{medmnistv2} contains 1{,}742 abdominal CT organ
volumes of size $28 \times 28 \times 28$ voxels with 11 organ classes
(train/val/test = 971/161/610). Volumes are \llm{M04c}{normalised}{See M04b.} to $[0, 1]$.
Aug.\ CNN is trained with one of the 24 octahedral rotations per sample;
equivariant models are trained without augmentation. Kernel rotations are implemented as exact
voxel permutations on the $3^3$ grid, avoiding interpolation artifacts. Subsets with
$n < 971$ are uniform random subsets, drawn per seed.


\paragraph{Spherical MNIST.}
MNIST digits are projected onto the sphere via stereographic
projection following \citet{cohen2018spherical} and sampled on a
$64 \times 128$ equiangular grid. The augmented selective bispectrum is
computed at \llm{M05b}{$\ell_{\max} = 15$}{See M05a. (A) $L{=}15$.}, producing 380 invariants, stored as complex numbers. The model input is obtained by applying
$\operatorname{sign}(x)\log(1+|x|)$ separately to the real and
imaginary parts of each coefficient, giving 760 real features. These features are
fed to a 3-layer MLP: Linear(760, 256)--BN--ReLU--Linear(256,
128)--BN--ReLU--Linear(128, 10). The power spectrum MLP uses the
same structure with hidden width 128: Linear(16, 128)--BN--ReLU--Linear(128,
64)--BN--ReLU--Linear(64, 10). The standard CNN has 4 convolutional
layers (16--32--64--64 channels) followed by adaptive average pooling
and a Linear(1024, 128)--ReLU--Linear(128, 10) classifier. S2CNN+BN is the spherical CNN of
\citet{cohen2018spherical} (features 20 and 40, bandwidths 10 and 6) with batch normalization
after each convolution, 155K parameters. The tensor-product network is an $\SO(3)$-equivariant
Clebsch--Gordan network with 24 channels per degree up to $\ell_{\max} = 6$, 386K parameters.
Latency is the median end-to-end forward time, from the spherical grid to the logits, on an idle
H100.

\section{Changes Since Submission}
\label{app:changes}

Every experiment in this version was re-run after the author response, when we found bugs in the library, the training protocol, and the pooling head.
Several numbers therefore differ from the submitted version and from the author response; this
section records what changed and why.

\subsection{Library (\texttt{bispectrum} 0.4.0)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{SO3onS2}.} For $\ell \ge 8$ the chain and cross rows of the selection
    encoded the same real equation (Remark~\ref{rem:app-Tell-rank-deficit}), so the linear rows
    had rank at most $\ell + 1 < 2\ell + 1$ and, even with the self-couplings and CG-power
    entries, the per-degree Jacobian fell short of full rank by $\lfloor(\ell-6)/2\rfloor$ at
    every $\ell \ge 8$ (20 dimensions in total at $L{=}15$); identically zero entries were also
    kept. The corrected selection has full per-degree rank, checked numerically
    (Section~\ref{sec:so3-selective}), and 380 coefficients at
    $L{=}15$ (submitted: 384) and 426 at $L{=}16$ (submitted: 430). The submitted
    reconstruction figure used the rank-deficient map.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{DnonDn}.} The fold block was dropped for odd $n \ge 5$, leaving Jacobian
    rank $2n - 1$, so the map was not locally injective. Odd $n$ gain the missing coefficients;
    even $n$, including the $D_{32}$ benchmark, are unchanged.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{SO2onDisk}.} The Bessel zeros were off by ${\sim}5 \times 10^{-7}$ and
    the basis had condition number ${\sim}10^{16}$. The default band limit is now the Nyquist
    limit, which changes the disk row of Table~\ref{tab:benchmarks} from 105 to 76 coefficients.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training protocol}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sample floor.} An epoch was one pass over the $n$ training examples, so at
    $n{=}100$ an epoch was about two optimizer steps. Every epoch now draws at least 10{,}000
    examples. This changes every low-data result; the Spherical MNIST bispectrum at $n{=}100$
    goes from 11.7\% to 62.7\%.
  \item \textbf{Fixed epochs.} Early stopping fired on the accuracy dips at SGDR warm restarts,
    and batch sizes differed between models. Runs now train for a fixed number of epochs ending
    a cosine cycle, at one batch size per dataset (tuned per model on Spherical MNIST), and are
    evaluated at the best-validation
    checkpoint (Appendix~\ref{app:exp}).
  \item \textbf{Also changed.} Aug.\ CNN is trained with augmentation at every training-set size;
    the rotated test is the primary metric; norm pooling is added as an incomplete-invariant
    baseline; we use 10, 3--5 and 5 seeds on OrganMNIST3D, PCam and Spherical MNIST, report the
    sample standard deviation, and test differences with Welch $t$-tests; and S2CNN and a
    tensor-product network are re-run under our protocol with matched tuning
    (Table~\ref{tab:smnist-results}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pooling head}

The submitted pooling head computed $\mathrm{proj}(\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{signlog}(\beta)))$,
which zeroes every negative $G$-bispectral coefficient before the $1{\times}1$ projection. The
camera-ready head computes $\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathrm{proj}(\mathrm{signlog}(\beta)))$; it was chosen
on validation AUC alone (Table~\ref{tab:head-ablation}). Spherical MNIST never used this head.
On $O$ the truncated invariant keeps full Jacobian rank
(200 of 200 random inputs) yet costs ${\approx}5$\,pp, while on $C_8$ it is rank-deficient
(24 of 200) yet costs nothing measurable, so Jacobian rank does not explain the gap. We suspect that the
surviving subset changes with every input, so the fixed projection reads different coordinates
at every voxel, and the zeroed entries pass no gradient. The rule we adopt is to apply only fixed
injective maps (real--imaginary split, signed log) before the first learned layer. Because we compared four heads for the bispectrum but only one per baseline,
Table~\ref{tab:head-ablation} also reports the submitted head. Norm pooling has no truncated variant, because its outputs are
non-negative and a ReLU leaves them unchanged.

\subsection{Errata}

The following statements from the submission or the author response no longer hold.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{OrganMNIST3D at 6.3M parameters.} We attributed max pooling's lead to an
    optimization or capacity limitation of the bispectral model (best regularized 72.5\% vs.\
    76.7\%). The cause was the pooling head. With the corrected head and 10 seeds, bispectral pooling
    reaches 77.6\% vs.\ 76.0\% ($p{=}0.10$). Likewise, the submitted tie with max pooling at
    0.46M parameters (72.6\% vs.\ 73.0\%) becomes 78.5\% vs.\ 73.6\% ($p < 10^{-3}$).
  \item \textbf{Spherical feature ablation.} We stated that each augmentation stage changes
    accuracy by more than the cross-seed variability. Only the Clebsch--Gordan power spectra do
    (Table~\ref{tab:smnist-augmentation}).
  \item \textbf{Reconstruction of random signals at $L{=}15$.} We reported gradient descent
    plateauing just above the acceptance threshold, with 3 of 16 random signals below it. On the
    corrected invariant gradient descent stalls at a 7.5\% median invariant error with 0 of 16
    below it, and the image-space residual we reported measured the rotation aligner rather than
    the invariant. The degree-by-degree solver recovers all 16 random signals
    (Section~\ref{sec:so3-recon}, Appendix~\ref{app:so3-recon}).
  \item \textbf{CPU latency.} We reported 0.082--402\,ms on x86. That run used one thread per core
    on a shared 128-core host; with the thread count fixed, every module except
    \texttt{SO3onS2} is sub-millisecond and \texttt{SO3onS2} takes 5.5--45\,ms on x86
    (Table~\ref{tab:portability}).
  \item \textbf{Spherical MNIST against the power spectrum.} We reported 95.0\% vs.\ 79.2\% for a
    matched-capacity power spectrum. The numbers are now 95.8\% (tuned) vs.\ 77.7\% at 11K
    parameters and 80.1\% at 550K (Appendix~\ref{app:smnist-capacity}); the conclusion holds.
  \item \textbf{Counts.} The spherical classifier has 760 input features and 229{,}770
    parameters (submitted: 768 and 231{,}818), and the test coverage of the library is 98.5\%
    (submitted: 96.8\%).
\end{itemize}
